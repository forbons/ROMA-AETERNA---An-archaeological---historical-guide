This morning, I went to mass in San Bernardo alle Terme, a Cistercian and titular church of the cardinal priest George Allencherry. In 1602 it has been dedicated to Saint Bernard of Clairvaux (ca. 1090-1153). He was the first abbot of Clairvaux, contributed notably to the dissemination of the Cistercian order and became famous for his literary and diplomatic activities. Thus, he was involved in the reform of the papacy and the propaganda for the crusades at that time. Another not-so-fun fact: Saint Bernard was against body care and hygiene because he believed this would damage one's soul. However, the patron saint of this church could also be Saint Bernard, who is the patron saint of mountaineers and people who live in the Alps.
The early modern church is built using the preserved core structure of the north-west tower of the Baths of Diocletian (298-306). The core of the ancient dome is also preserved and it somewhat resembles the Pantheon, also allowing light to brighten the inside of the church through a - in this case - partially covered oculus. In antiquity, the room was presumably used for sport activities or relaxing.
In this chapel that is connected to the main room, there is the grave of the German painter Friedrich Overbeck.
